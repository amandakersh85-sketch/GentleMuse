# Instagram eligibility for Club Target — the 500-follower rule does not hold

**Established 2026-10-05 by Amanda, first-hand. This supersedes every earlier statement in
this repo that Instagram cannot earn Club Target points.**

## The finding

**An Instagram STORY submission WAS CREDITED for points.**

Her Instagram `@thegentlemuse2026` was read live the same day at **208 followers** — far below
Target's stated 500 floor. She did not grow past the gate. The gate did not behave the way
this repo has been asserting since 30 August.

## What this changes

**Submit Instagram challenges. Lead with Stories.**

Roughly **30 of the ~40 board items are Instagram**, including **13 IG Story challenges**. All
of them were being treated as worth nothing. They are live targets.

Do not deprioritise an Instagram cut any more. Note also that the objective is follower
growth, not the scoreboard, so Instagram was never a waste even when it earned nothing — the
points are now upside on top of that.

## What is established vs. what is not

**Established:** a Story credited at 208 followers.

**NOT established — do not assert either way:**
- whether IG Reels and feed posts also credit under 500, or
- whether Stories are specifically exempt from the follower floor.

**Submit both, then record which ones credit.** Closing that question is the point of this
file. Write the answer here when the evidence arrives.

## IG Story claims are same-day or never

A Story challenge needs a **screenshot of the live Story**, and Stories expire after 24 hours.
So a Story claim has to happen the day it posts or the evidence is gone. Treat an unclaimed
live Story as more urgent than any feed post.

Never fabricate or mock up that screenshot. It is evidence of a real published Story.

## Why this was wrong for six weeks, and the transferable lesson

"Posting from an account with under 500 followers" **is** printed in Target's own rejection
list, and a real rejection flood on 2026-09-22 lined up with it exactly. The floor was treated
as proven because a documented rule and an observed failure pointed the same direction.

They were a hypothesis, not a finding. **A stated criterion plus a correlated failure is not
proof of causation** — especially when the conclusion is "stop working a whole channel."
A rule that shuts down 30 of 40 board items deserves one real test before it becomes canon.
