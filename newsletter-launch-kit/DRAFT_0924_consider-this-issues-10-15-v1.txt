SUBJECT: Your dishwasher has a filter
PREVIEW TEXT: Most people have never taken it out.

---

If your dishwasher was made in roughly the last 15 years, there is a filter in the floor of
it, under the bottom rack, and it comes out by hand.

Older machines ground food up with a small motor. Newer ones are quieter and use less water,
and the tradeoff is that they catch food in a filter instead. The filter is usually a plastic
cylinder you twist a quarter turn to release, sometimes over a flat screen that lifts out
with it.

Nobody tells you this at delivery. So the filter fills, water starts moving through a layer
of old food on its way to the spray arms, and the machine begins doing the opposite of its
job. The signs arrive one at a time and none of them look like a filter: glasses that dry
cloudy, grit on the inside of a bowl, a smell that comes back 2 days after you clean the door
seal.

Do this once, tonight, with the machine empty.

Pull out the bottom rack. Find the round piece in the middle of the floor. Twist, lift, take
it to the sink, rinse it under hot water with an old toothbrush. Put it back and turn it
until it seats. It takes 3 minutes.

Then do it monthly if you rinse plates, every 2 weeks if you do not.

If there is no cylinder down there, you have the older grinding kind and this one is not
yours. Check the manual before you go looking for a part that was never installed.

---

SUBJECT: Your smoke alarm has an expiration date
PREVIEW TEXT: It is printed on the back. The battery is not the part that expires.

INTERNAL: Placed here on purpose. Clocks go back Sunday 11/01, and the whole country is
already being told to change the batteries that weekend. This issue is the part that message
leaves out.

---

Everyone knows to change the battery when the clocks change. Almost nobody knows the alarm
itself expires.

The sensor inside degrades whether it ever sees smoke or not. 10 years from the date of
manufacture and the unit is done, and a fresh battery does not restore anything. It just
means a dead sensor now has power.

The date is printed on the back of the alarm, on the flat side against the ceiling. Not the
front. You have to take it down to read it.

So this weekend, when you are already on the step stool:

Twist the alarm off the bracket. Turn it over. Find the manufacture date. If it is 2016 or
older, that alarm is finished and the replacement is the whole unit, not a battery. Write the
date you replaced it on the new one in marker before you install it, because in 10 years you
will not remember either.

While you are holding it, press the test button. If the sound is weak or it takes a moment to
start, that is the same conversation.

Carbon monoxide alarms expire faster, usually 5 to 10 years, and theirs is printed the same
way. Combination units follow the shorter clock.

This is the least interesting thing in your house and it is the only item on this list where
being 2 years late has a consequence you cannot undo.

SUBJECT: Your fridge is running longer than it used to
PREVIEW TEXT: There is a dusty part underneath doing all the work.

---

The refrigerator has a set of coils that dump heat out of the box and into your kitchen.
Depending on the model they are behind a removable grille at the bottom front, or flat
against the back.

They are the one part of the appliance that is designed to be dirty, and they collect
everything: dust, hair, whatever the dog contributes. A coated coil cannot release heat as
well, so the compressor runs longer to hit the same temperature. Nothing breaks. It just
works harder, every hour, for years.

The tell is not a warm fridge. It is a fridge you can hear more often than you used to, and a
kitchen floor that is warm in one spot.

Once or twice a year, and twice if you have pets:

Unplug it. Pull off the bottom grille, which usually snaps or unclips rather than unscrews.
Vacuum what you can reach with the crevice tool, then go in with a coil brush, which is a
long thin brush that costs a few dollars and exists for this exact job. Vacuum up what the
brush pulls forward. Plug it back in.

If your coils are on the back, you will have to walk the fridge out to reach them. Do that on
a day you were already cleaning behind it.

Wipe the door gasket while you are there, but do not confuse the 2 jobs. The gasket is about
what gets in. The coils are about how hard the motor has to work to fix it.

---

SUBJECT: Check the number on your water heater
PREVIEW TEXT: It probably left the factory hotter than it needs to be.

---

Water heaters commonly ship set around 140 degrees. The Department of Energy recommends 120
for most households.

That 20 degree difference does 2 things. It is the difference between hot water and water
that scalds a child or an older adult faster than they can pull their hand back, and it is
heat you pay to maintain in a tank all day whether anyone showers or not.

On an electric heater the dial is behind an access panel, sometimes 2, and you cut power at
the breaker before you open anything. On a gas heater it is usually a dial on the control
valve at the bottom front, often marked with letters or hash marks rather than numbers.

If yours is unmarked, run the hot tap at the kitchen sink for 2 minutes, fill a cup, and put
a cooking thermometer in it. That reading is your answer.

Turn it down in small moves and live with it for a few days before you decide.

2 exceptions worth knowing. Households with someone immunocompromised sometimes keep a higher
setting on medical advice, because bacteria grow more readily in warm standing water. And if
your dishwasher has no internal heater, very low settings can leave dishes poorly washed.

While you are down there, look at the age. Most tanks last 8 to 12 years, and the date is
usually encoded in the serial number on the label. A tank you are not sure about is worth
knowing the age of before a cold January morning tells you.

---

SUBJECT: Hold a square of toilet paper to your bathroom fan
PREVIEW TEXT: If it falls, the fan is decoration.

---

Here is a 10 second test.

Turn the bathroom fan on. Tear off 1 square of toilet paper and hold it flat against the
grille. A working fan holds it there. A fan that lets it drop is spinning, making noise and
moving almost nothing.

The 2 usual reasons are both out of sight. The duct above the ceiling has come loose from the
fan housing or the roof cap, which happens more often than anyone expects, and the fan is
politely venting your shower into the attic. Or the exterior flap is stuck shut with paint,
lint or a nest.

The second thing people get wrong is the timing. The fan's job is not to run during the
shower, it is to run after it. Give it 15 to 20 minutes once you are out. A basic timer
switch handles that for you and costs less than the paint job the moisture ruins.

What failure looks like, in order: the mirror takes longer to clear than it used to, the
paint near the ceiling starts to look soft, then the corner above the shower goes gray and
you are cleaning the same spot every month and blaming the cleaner.

If the square drops, put a flashlight in the attic before you buy a new fan. The fan is
usually fine.

SUBJECT: Your range hood filter goes in the dishwasher
PREVIEW TEXT: Today of all days.

INTERNAL: This lands on Thanksgiving morning. That is deliberate and the copy leans on it,
but lightly. If Amanda would rather it not send on a holiday, move it to 12/03 and the run
just ends 1 week later.

---

You are about to run that hood harder than any other day of the year, so take 2 minutes
before the oven goes on.

The metal mesh panels under the range hood slide or unclip out by hand. They are grease
filters, they are almost always dishwasher safe, and they are meant to be washed. A saturated
mesh stops catching grease and starts shedding it, which is how the underside of the hood and
the cabinet doors beside the stove end up sticky without anyone spilling anything.

Pull them, run them on the top rack, put them back. If they come out heavy and amber, soak
them first in hot water with dish soap and a scoop of baking soda.

Now the part most people never learn. Hoods come in 2 kinds. A vented hood pushes air
outside. A recirculating hood pulls air through a charcoal filter and blows it back into the
kitchen, and that charcoal filter is not washable. It is replaced, roughly every 6 months
with normal cooking, and there are kitchens running 5 year old charcoal that is doing nothing
at all.

To find out which you have, turn the fan on high and check whether air is leaving the house
at the vent outside. No exterior vent means recirculating, which means there is a charcoal
filter behind or above the mesh, and it has a model number on it.

The mesh is today's job. The charcoal is the thing to order this weekend.
