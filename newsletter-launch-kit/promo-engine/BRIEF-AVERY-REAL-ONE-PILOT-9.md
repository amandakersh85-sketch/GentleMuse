---

## 1. What you are actually working on

Amanda runs The Gentle Muse. From **Oct 1 2026 to Jan 1 2027** she is publishing **1 true thing
about the season every single night for 93 consecutive nights.** The campaign is called
**The Real One.**

**The premise, and everything hangs off this:** every holiday in that window has a version
everyone repeats and a version that actually happened, and the second one is better. Trick or
treating was invented as crowd control. The plague doctor mask is 300 years too late for the
Black Death. Nobody was burned at Salem. Giving thanks at harvest was already here before the
Pilgrims landed.

**It is not spooky content and it is not festive content. It is correction content**, delivered
calmly by someone who went and checked. Every fact is verified against source and the source is
named before anything is written.

**Who is watching:** mostly women over 40. They follow because they enjoy the facts, not because
they want to be sold to. Amanda's line on this: *"we don't want to over commercialize it."*

**Why the visuals matter more than usual:** this runs 93 nights in a row. If night 12 and night
77 do not look like the same publication, it stops being a series and goes back to being posts.
Your job is to make 93 nights feel like one body of work.

## 2. The visual system

### Style
Hand-inked engraving, in the manner of a 19th century natural history or almanac plate. Visible
line work and cross-hatching. Flat printing. **No** photographic depth of field, **no** 3D
render, **no** glossy digital lighting, **no** lens flare, **no** bokeh.

### Palette, exact, every plate, no exceptions
| Role | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | `#F2EBDD` | aged bone paper, full background |
| Ink | `#1B1830` | deep aubergine-black, all line work |
| Ember | `#C2552B` | burnt amber. **Exactly 1 element per plate** |
| Moss | `#4F7A5C` | muted green, secondary, optional |

4 colours. If a plate seems to need a 5th, the composition is wrong. Fix the composition.

### Composition, every plate
- **1 subject, centred**, occupying roughly the middle 60%.
- A **thin single ruled border** inset 6% from every edge, in Ink, 2px at 1080 wide.
- Real empty paper between subject and border. Do not fill the frame.
- **A clear horizontal band across the bottom 12%, left empty.** See section 3.

### NO TEXT. AT ALL. ANYWHERE.
No titles, no captions, no labels, no dates, no numbers, no signage lettering, no readable book
spines. **Not even the series name.** Where a subject would naturally carry writing, draw it as
illegible marks.

This is not a style preference. It is how the text stays perfect across 93 nights. See next.

### Sizes
- **1080 x 1350 (4:5)** — primary. Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, YouTube.
- **1080 x 1920 (9:16)** — Pinterest. Recompose at the new ratio. Do not stretch or crop.

### Naming
`realone-LEG-NN-slug-4x5.png` and `realone-LEG-NN-slug-9x16.png`
LEG is `oct`, `nov` or `dec`. NN is 01 to 03.

---

## 3. Why you leave the bottom band empty

Every plate carries a footer:

```
THE REAL ONE · 11 NIGHTS TO HALLOWEEN
```

The number changes nightly. **Claude composites that footer on afterwards with real type**, so
it is pixel-identical on all 93 nights and always legible. Image models mangle lettering and a
countdown that is wrong on one night is worse than no countdown.

**So: you make the art, Claude adds the text.** Leave the bottom 12% as clean paper, inside the
ruled border. No subject matter in it, no cross-hatching, nothing the footer has to fight.

---

## 4. The 3 legs, and what each one has to feel like

The palette and style never change. What changes is weight.

### LEG 1 · OCTOBER — Halloween. `oct`
Sharpest and most graphic of the 3. High contrast, more empty paper, colder. This leg is about
things being **stranger than you were told.**

### LEG 2 · NOVEMBER — harvest and Thanksgiving. `nov`
Warmest and softest. More Moss, more organic shapes, more texture. This leg is about things
being **older than you were told.** It ends on the hardest week of the run, where the honest
history of Thanksgiving sits next to genuine gratitude, so the plates must not be glib.

### LEG 3 · DECEMBER — midwinter and Christmas. `dec`
Densest and most ornamental, but still restrained. More line work, more interior and firelight
subjects, the darkest Ink values of the 3. This leg is about things being **borrowed, invented
or sold**, and almost none of it starting where people think.

**A stranger should be able to sort the 9 into 3 piles without being told the rule, and still
see all 9 as one publication.** That is the test this pilot exists to run.

## 5. The 9 plates

These were chosen to **stress the system**, not because they are the prettiest. Each leg gets a
simple object, a complex scene, and a modern subject. If the spec breaks anywhere, it breaks on
one of these, which is exactly what a pilot is for.

### October
| # | Slug | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | `plague-mask` | A beaked plague doctor mask resting face-up on a bare table, dried lavender and a small glass vial beside it. Ember on the lavender. *(simple object, high contrast)* |
| 02 | `danse-macabre` | A procession in profile: a crowned figure, a robed figure, a farmer and a child walking in a line, with skeletal figures between them. Ember on the crown. *(complex multi-figure, the hardest composition in the run)* |
| 03 | `dead-retail` | The exterior of a large empty big-box store at dusk, sign board blank, parking lot vacant. Ember in 1 lit window. *(modern subject: does the engraving style survive something contemporary)* |

### November
| # | Slug | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | `thirteen-moons` | A row of 13 moons across the plate in their phases, each rendered as an engraved disc, with a fine horizontal rule beneath them. Ember on 1 moon. *(minimal and repetitive: tests restraint and consistency)* |
| 02 | `three-sisters` | Corn, beans and squash growing together in a single mound, the beans climbing the corn stalk, squash leaves spread at the base. Ember on the squash flower. *(organic and warm: the Moss leg at its most characteristic)* |
| 03 | `the-campaign` | A writing desk with a tall stack of sealed letters, an inkwell and a single pen laid across the top letter. No readable writing. Ember on the topmost seal. *(Sarah Josepha Hale spent decades writing letters to get Thanksgiving made a holiday. Tests whether a quiet, abstract, human subject reads.)* |

### December
| # | Slug | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | `solstice` | A low winter sun just above a bare horizon, a single standing stone in the foreground casting a long shadow. Ember on the sun. *(most minimal plate in the set: tests whether the system holds with almost nothing in it)* |

| 03 | `wish-book` | A thick mail-order catalogue lying open on a table, pages fanned, a child's hand-drawn circle around 1 unreadable item on the page. ## 6. How to run it

**Do not batch all 9.** The failure mode on a long series is drift nobody notices until it is
finished.

1. Generate **oct-01 `plague-mask`** first, at 4:5. Check it against section 7. Fix until it
   passes. **That approved plate is the reference for everything after it.**
2. Then the remaining 8, one at a time. Check each one **side by side with oct-01**, never alone.
3. When a plate passes at 4:5, generate its 9:16.
4. After all 9, lay them out as a single row and look at it. Then sort them into 3 piles by leg
   without looking at the filenames. If you cannot, the legs are not distinct enough, and say so
   rather than handing over 9 plates that all look the same.

---

## 7. Checklist. A plate ships only if every line is yes.

1. Background is `#F2EBDD`, edge to edge.
2. Exactly 4 colours, and they are the 4 specified.
3. Ember appears on **exactly 1** element.
4. Thin ruled border, inset, unbroken, Ink.
5. **Bottom 12% is clean empty paper inside the border.**
6. **Zero readable characters anywhere in the image.**
7. Engraving line work. Not photographic, not 3D, not painterly.
8. 1 clear subject, centred, with real empty paper around it.
9. Next to oct-01, it reads as the same hand and the same book.
10. Nothing gruesome. Bones, graves and masks are fine as period illustration. No gore, no
    blood, no corpses, no distressed human faces. Several of these cover real deaths of real
    named people.
