# Why reach collapsed, and what actually moved it

Pulled 2026-08-30 from Blotato post analytics (Instagram, 25 posts, Jul 25 to Aug 30) and
Metricool best-time data for @thegentlemuse2026, timezone America/Chicago.

## The one post that worked

| Post | Views | Reach | Shares | Comments | Likes | Saves |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `6250675` Aug 18 | **7,726** | **7,109** | **54** | 45 | 731 | 20 |
| `6096598` Aug 13 | 1,865 | 1,289 | 4 | 0 | 58 | 0 |
| `6230969` Aug 18 | 1,842 | 1,261 | 2 | 0 | 32 | 0 |
| `6373662` Aug 23 | 1,818 | 1,455 | 0 | 17 | 71 | 1 |

The winner did 4.1x the next best. Its caption:

> Coming home to a 19 year old Chihuahua after a long shift. She does not care how the day
> went or what got done. She just wants me back. Comment CESA and follow @cesasgoldenyears.

280 characters. Three sentences and a CTA.

**Shares are the mechanism.** 54 of them, against 9 for the next highest post in the whole
set. Its snapshot history shows why that matters: 3,226 views at 24 hours, still climbing to
7,726 by day 7. Nothing else in the set grew after day 1. A post with shares keeps getting
re-served. A post without them is finished in a day.

What gets shared is a sentence someone wants to send to a specific person. "She just wants me
back" is sendable. "She peed in my office and then looked me dead in the eye" is funny and
nobody forwards it. Worth noting that `6279555` literally asked, "Send this to someone whose
dog helped raise her," and got 1 share and 192 views. The ask does not produce shares. The
feeling does.

## Reach collapsed after Aug 20

Comparing views at roughly 24 hours old, so recency is not doing the work:

| Window | Typical 24h views |
|---|---|
| Aug 13 to 20 | 1,250 to 3,226 |
| Aug 22 to 29 | 159 to 790 |

Roughly a 7x drop, across every content type, not just the dog posts.

**It is not an engagement problem.** Recent posts run 9 to 13 percent engagement on the reach
they get, which is healthy. `6533423` got 16 interactions on 126 reach. The content is
landing on the people who see it. Instagram just stopped showing it to anyone new. Reach of
126 to 195 against roughly 100 followers means she is reaching her own followers and almost
nobody else.

## What changed in the captions

The winner was 280 characters. The posts published after Aug 26 run 480 to 640 characters and
most of them pitch the guide inside the caption: "13 pages, plain words, comfort not
medicine. Free. Comment CESA and I'll send it over."

Short posts in this set (under 300 characters) have a median around 1,000 views. Long ones
(over 450) sit near 210.

**Stated honestly: length is confounded with date.** Most of the long captions are also the
recent ones, so some of that gap is whatever else changed after Aug 20. The cleanest single
comparison available is `6536249` on Aug 28, which reused a proven video with a short caption
and pulled 1,590 in 24 hours while everything else that week sat at 179 to 239. That is
suggestive, not conclusive.

## Simultaneous posting was cannibalizing reach

Reels were repeatedly published to the same account within the same minute:

| Date | Posts | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Aug 18 00:00 | `6230969` + `6231222` | 1,842 and 427 |
| Aug 23 00:00 | `6373662` + `6373653` | 1,818 and **162** |
| Aug 24 00:00 | `6399066` + `6399174` | 159 and 225 |

A new Reel gets a small test audience. Two at once split it and the second one dies. On
Aug 23 the two posts went out 2 seconds apart and one got 11x the other.

## The conversion number nobody wants

`6250675` carried "Comment CESA" and drew **45 comments. Not one of them was CESA.** They
were all about the commenter's own dog: "Senior chihuahuas are the best," "my Miku only got
to see 12," "yours is my 15 year old's twin."

That is the whole conversion problem in one post. The caption asked two things at once, and
people did the easier, more natural one. When a post invites stories, it gets stories. The
keyword has to be the only ask in the post, or it loses to the conversation every time.

# Caption surgery, 2026-08-30

Amanda approved cutting the 6 long Instagram captions to the winning shape. Graded through
post-grader first. No voice violations in any of them: no em dashes, no hype, digits used
throughout, 4 hashtags each. Every defect was structural.

| Post | Before | Hook | Body+CTA | The actual defect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `3824812` TUESDAY | 6/10 | 2.5 | 3.5 | Opened on "today's issue went out," a warm-up about the newsletter instead of the story |
| `3802715` CONSIDER | 6.5/10 | 4 | 2.5 | **2 competing asks.** Comment CONSIDER, and also send this to whoever owns the sponge |
| `3824817` GUIDE | 6.5/10 | 3 | 3.5 | The line that lands, "It was 5 days old," sat in paragraph 3 |
| `3824799` TUESDAY | 7/10 | 4.5 | 2.5 | Strong hook, then the payoff buried under an explainer |
| `3836037` CONSIDER | 7.5/10 | 5 | 2.5 | Best hook in the queue, then DM copy pasted into the caption |
| `3836046` CONSIDER | 7.5/10 | 4.5 | 3 | Same, and it holds the Wednesday peak slot |

**The single pattern behind all 6:** every one explained the newsletter inside the caption.
That is what the DM is for. The caption's only job is to earn the comment.

Cut from 616 to 699 characters down to 290 to 390. One ask each. The share prompt on
`3802715` was removed on purpose: `6279555` asked outright for shares and got 1, while the
7,726 view post asked for nothing and got 54. The explicit ask does not produce shares and it
does cost the keyword.

## A caveat worth recording

The 280 character finding comes from Reels. All 25 posts in the analytics pull were video.
4 of these 6 are static or carousel posts, where the caption carries more of the load. The
parts that transfer cleanly are one ask instead of 2, hook before the truncation point, and
no lead magnet explainer. Pure length is the softest of the 3 claims.

# Wrong-destination sweep, 2026-08-30

Reading the far end of the queue for the caption work turned up a separate problem: offer copy
pointing at the wrong landing page. The Reset Guide payhip link had become a catch-all.

| `3939641` YT | Sep 23 | Press Play | payhip Reset | press-play |

`3797034` is the one that mattered most. It carried a `preview.mailerlite.io` link, which is
explicitly on the DO NOT USE list in START_HERE, and it was set to fire Aug 31. The August
sweep missed it because that sweep read post text and this link lived in `firstComment`.
**Any future link audit has to read firstComment and the target link field, not just text.**

Both LinkedIn posts also said "Comment CONSIDER," which cannot fire there. LinkedIn has no
keyword mechanism, so per KEYWORD-RULE it carries the direct link instead. Fixed.

`3939593` had no call to action of any kind. A guide promo with no keyword and no link.
