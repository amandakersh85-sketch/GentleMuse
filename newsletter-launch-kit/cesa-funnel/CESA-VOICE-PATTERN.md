## The 6 moves, in order

1. **Open on something you can see, not something you claim.** "She checks the whole yard,
   patch by patch." A physical observation, present tense, no thesis. The hook is that the
   reader is now watching something.
2. **Stack dense sensory specifics.** Pink harness. Leash trailing. Nose down. Dandelions
   open. Sun and shade. Concrete nouns doing the work, no adjectives reaching for feeling.
3. **Say out loud that nothing is happening.** "Nothing really happens here." This is the
   move that separates it from every other senior dog account. It removes the threat of a
   sad twist, so people stay.
4. **One small beat at the end, earned not announced.** "stops where the grass runs out and
   looks straight up at me." The feeling lives in the image. It is never named.
5. **Soft CTA matched to the channel.** On her TikTok that is follow. Not a pitch.
6. **5 niche hashtags.** Specific over big. #dandelions and #slowwalks over #dogs.

---

## What it never does

- **Hands out a lesson.** No "here's what she taught me about..." The reader draws it.
- **Editorializes the dog's feelings.** She does things. She does not "know how much I
  needed her today."
- **Leads with age or mortality as the hook.** 19 shows up as a detail inside the piece,
  never as the opening bid for sympathy.
- **Uses the clip as a pretext.** The caption describes what is actually on screen. Captions
  that describe a different moment than the video get caught and they read as fake.

## The failure mode this replaced

The first pass at these captions opened with claims and closed with takeaways. Example, the
original "grow old" caption:

> Some dogs don't just grow old beside you. They quietly love you through every version of
> yourself you had to become.
> ...
> She never needed the version of me that had it together. That is the part I think about now.

Opens on a thesis, closes by pointing at the moral. Rewritten to the pattern:

> The floor changes in this. She doesn't.
>
> Different rooms, different jobs, different stretches I did not talk about out loud. Same
> small dog on the floor in the same 3 feet, chin down, waiting for me to be done with
> whatever it was.
>
> She has been here for 10 of her 19 years. She was never impressed by any of it.
>
> Nothing happens in this clip. She lies down. I keep going. That was the whole arrangement.

Same content. The reader gets to do the work.

## Adapting per channel

The 6 moves stay. Only the ending changes, and the ending is set by the channel's job
(see `promo-engine/ROTATION-TEST.md`, Cesa section).

| Channel | Ending |
|---|---|
| Cesa TikTok | "Follow along for more of her." Bio link only once the bio is live. |
| Cesa Instagram | Comment CESA, or CONSIDER on newsletter days. Keyword always. |
| Amanda Instagram | Introduce her first ("This is Cesa. I have had 10 of her 19 years."), the audience does not know her. Then comment CESA. |
| Amanda Facebook | Same, longer. Name the reader's problem before the offer. This is the senior dog demographic. |

On Amanda's channels the middle gains one paragraph the Cesa-channel version does not need:
the bridge from the scene to why the guide exists. Keep it factual. "Accidents at this age
are usually not defiance. Most of it is bladder capacity, floor traction, and how far the
door is. All 3 are adjustable." That paragraph is the conversion, not the CTA line.
