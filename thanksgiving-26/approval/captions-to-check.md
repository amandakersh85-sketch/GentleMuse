## 1. Sun 11/01, pre-1500s

**Marigolds are there to be smelled, not seen.**

Cempasuchil, from the Nahuatl cempoalli for 20 and xochitl for flower. On Dia de los Muertos the petals are torn up and laid in a path from the door to the altar, because it is the scent and not the colour that is believed to bring the dead in. The Aztecs grew it for this long before the Spanish arrived.

*The path is not decoration. It is a scent trail, laid for somebody expected.*

Sound: a single guitar, no ensemble

- [Mexico News Daily, cempasuchil: the Nahuatl cempoalli and xochitl, and the petal path laid from the door to the altar](https://mexiconewsdaily.com/culture/celebrating-cempasuchil-day-of-the-day-marigolds-after-day-of-the-dead/)

## 2. Mon 11/02, pre-1500s

**It is 2 days, and the children come first.**

November 1 is Dia de los Angelitos, the day of the little angels, held for children who died. November 2 is for the adults. The altar is built for different people on different days, and what goes on it changes to match: toys and sweets on the 1st, pan de muerto and tequila on the 2nd.

*It is not a long weekend. It is 2 different guest lists.*

Sound: a single guitar, no ensemble

- Catholic All Saints and All Souls calendar merged with Mexican practice; widely documented in regional custom

---

## 3. Tue 11/03, no date

**The harvest moon is named after overtime.**

Most of the year the moon rises about 45 minutes later each night. Around the autumn equinox the ecliptic sits at a shallow angle to the horizon and the gap shrinks to 25 or 30 minutes for several nights running, so there is no dark hour between sunset and moonrise. They worked through it.

*It is not brighter. It is just punctual.*

Sound: warm modern instrumental, open and slow

- the shallow angle of the ecliptic to the horizon at the autumnal equinox; standard astronomical explanation

## 4. Wed 11/04, pre-1400s

**Harvest used to mean the season, not the work.**

The Old English haerfest meant autumn itself, the season, with the gathering only implied. The crop sense takes over by about 1500, and the season has to borrow a name: autumn from Latin by way of French, and fall, which is short for fall of the leaf.

*The season lost its own name to the chore, and got called after falling leaves instead.*

Sound: one plucked string instrument, sparse

- Oxford English Dictionary entries for harvest and autumn; Old English haerfest

## 5. Thu 11/05, 1880s

**A good cranberry bounces.**

A ripe cranberry has air pockets inside, so it bounces, and a bruised one does not. John Webb worked it out in the 1880s in New Jersey: his wooden leg would not carry the crop down from the barn loft, so he poured it down the stairs and only the firm fruit reached the bottom. The bounce board separator came out of that, and growers still sort on it.

*A man who could not carry his own crop downstairs invented the machine that sorts it.*

Sound: scratchy period recording, late 1800s parlour

- the bounce board attributed to New Jersey grower John Webb; still the basis of modern separators

## 6. Fri 11/06, 1621

**There was no pie at the 1621 meal.**

Edward Winslow wrote the only eyewitness account. There was venison and wildfowl. There was no butter, no wheat flour and no oven in the colony, which is 3 of the things a pie needs. The bread was maize. The birds were stuffed with onions and herbs, not bread.

*There was nothing in Plymouth to bake a pie in.*

Sound: one plucked string instrument, sparse

- Edward Winslow's letter in Mourt's Relation, 1622

## 7. Sat 11/07, 1621

**It ran 3 days and most of the guests were Wampanoag.**

Winslow's letter of December 11, 1621, printed in Mourt's Relation, says Massasoit came with some ninetie men whom for three dayes we entertained and feasted. About 50 colonists were left alive to do the entertaining. The Wampanoag went out hunting and brought back 5 deer.

*The guests outnumbered the hosts 2 to 1 and brought most of the meat.*

Sound: one plucked string instrument, sparse

- Edward Winslow, letter of December 11, 1621, in Mourt's Relation, London 1622: "their greatest king Massasoyt, with some ninetie men whom for three dayes we entertained and feasted"

## 8. Sun 11/08, 1827 to 1863

**A magazine editor asked for 36 years and got a yes in 5 days.**

Sarah Josepha Hale started calling for a national day of thanksgiving in 1827 and began writing to presidents in 1846. Taylor, Fillmore, Pierce and Buchanan all declined. She wrote to Lincoln on September 28, 1863. He proclaimed it on October 3. She also wrote Mary Had a Little Lamb.

*36 years of being told no, and then 5 days.*

Sound: sparse piano, 1860s parlour

- Hale in Ladies Magazine from 1827 and Godey's Lady's Book from 1846; her letter to Lincoln dated September 28, 1863; the proclamation of October 3, 1863

## 9. Mon 11/09, 1939

**A president moved Thanksgiving and the country split over it.**

Roosevelt moved Thanksgiving a week earlier in 1939 to lengthen the Christmas shopping season. That year 23 states kept the old date, 22 took the new one, and Colorado, Mississippi and Texas kept both. Congress fixed it to the 4th Thursday in 1941.

*For 2 years there were 2 Thanksgivings, depending which side of a state line you woke up on.*

Sound: 1930s radio orchestra, slight hiss

- FDR's 1939 proclamation; the joint resolution of December 1941 setting the fourth Thursday

## 10. Tue 11/10, 1924

**The first Macy's Thanksgiving parade was not called that.**

The 1924 parade ran 6 miles from 145th Street with bears, elephants, camels and monkeys on loan from the Central Park Zoo, and Santa on a float of fake ice at the back. It was billed as the Macy's Christmas Parade. Balloons replaced the animals in 1927, which is the year the name changed too.

*It was a Christmas parade that happened to fall on Thanksgiving, and the name took 3 years to catch up.*

Sound: period brass band, scratchy

- Macy's parade history, 1924 first procession and 1927 introduction of balloons

---

## 11. Wed 11/11, 1928

**They used to let the balloons go on purpose.**

From 1928 the balloons were released on purpose at the end of the route. Tony Sarg built them to leak slowly, with a return address sewn on and 100 dollars for whoever sent one back. It stopped after 1932, when a pilot nearly crashed trying to catch one in the air.

*The end of the parade was a scavenger hunt held over New York.*

Sound: scratchy period recording, late 1920s dance band

- Macy's parade accounts of the balloon release years and the 1932 aircraft incident

## 12. Thu 11/12, 1500s

**The bird is American and the name is not.**

Turkish traders sold a West African bird into England first, and when colonists met the American bird they reused the name. Turkey itself calls it hindi, for India. France got dinde from poulet d'Inde, chicken of India. Portuguese sailors called it peru.

*Every country that named this bird named it after somewhere it has never been.*

Sound: one plucked string instrument, sparse

- Oxford English Dictionary on turkey; the Levant trade route attribution

## 13. Fri 11/13, no date

**Wild turkeys sleep in trees.**

A wild turkey flies up into a tree at dusk and spends the night there, off the ground and out of reach of anything that hunts on it. It can hit roughly 55 miles an hour in a short burst. The bird we eat is the same animal, bred heavy enough that it never gets up there.

*What we bred out of it was the way up.*

Sound: warm modern instrumental, open and slow

- state wildlife agency accounts of wild turkey roosting and burst flight speed; breed weight is why the domestic bird is flightless in practice

## 14. Sat 11/14, pre-500 BC

**Pulling the wishbone is about 2500 years old.**

The Etruscans read birds as oracles, down to scattering grain on letters of the alphabet and writing down the order a chicken pecked them in. They dried the furcula, the little fork in the breastbone, and stroked it for a wish. The breaking came later, when there were not enough bones to go round.

*2,500 years ago it was an oracle. Now it is 2 people and a plate.*

Sound: one plucked string instrument, sparse

- [InsideHook, the history of the wishbone: Etruscan alectryomancy, the furcula dried and stroked, and the Roman shortage that started the breaking](https://www.insidehook.com/article/history/history-wishbone-suggests-might-sex-thing)
- [The Etruscan link and alectryomancy are well attested for the 8th to 3rd century BCE. The drying and stroking detail comes from popular retellings and not from primary scholarship, so the caption says roughly 2,500 years old and stops](https://modernfarmer.com/?p=30659)

## 15. Sun 11/15, no date

**The turkey is not what makes you sleepy.**

Tryptophan sits at about the same level in all protein, and chicken, cheese and fish carry as much or more. It is also the rarest of the amino acids and it loses the queue for the transporter into the brain. Carbohydrates clear the competition out of the way, so it is the stuffing and the potatoes letting it through.

*You are not drugged. You are full.*

Sound: warm modern instrumental, open and slow

- nutritional comparisons of tryptophan content; the amino acid competition at the blood brain barrier

## 16. Mon 11/16, 1955

**Green bean casserole was written by a woman at a soup company in 1955.**

Dorcas Reilly supervised the Campbell's test kitchen in Camden and wrote it in 1955 out of 6 things already in the pantry: cream of mushroom soup, milk, soy sauce, pepper, green beans and fried onions. She called it Green Bean Bake.

*6 ingredients, every one of them already in the cupboard, and it has not changed since.*

Sound: 1950s light orchestral, soft brass

- Campbell's Soup Company records; the Reilly recipe card at the National Inventors Hall of Fame

## 17. Tue 11/17, 1876

**Thanksgiving football is older than the national holiday is settled.**

Yale beat Princeton 2 to 0 at Elysian Fields in Hoboken on November 30, 1876, in front of fewer than 1,000 people. That is 13 years after Lincoln's proclamation and 65 years before Congress put the date in law.

*Fewer than 1,000 people stood in a field in Hoboken and started the biggest television day of the year.*

Sound: period brass band, scratchy

- [Library of Congress, Thanksgiving football and the emergence of an American game](https://blogs.loc.gov/bibliomania/2025/11/25/thanksgiving-football-and-the-emergence-of-an-american-game/)
- [HISTORY, the first Thanksgiving college football game: Yale 2, Princeton 0, Elysian Fields in Hoboken, November 30, 1876, fewer than 1,000 in attendance](https://www.history.com/articles/thanksgiving-college-football-game-origins-princeton-yale)

## 18. Wed 11/18, 1973

**The Thanksgiving special serves toast and popcorn.**

The 1973 special serves toast, popcorn fried in a pan, pretzel sticks, jelly beans and sundaes, on a ping pong table in the back yard. Peppermint Patty, who invited herself, is furious about it, and Marcie has to explain to her why she has no standing to be.

*Toast and jelly beans on a ping pong table, and it is the Thanksgiving meal everybody can describe.*

Sound: warm jazz piano trio, newly played, not the original score

- A Charlie Brown Thanksgiving, CBS, November 1973

## 19. Thu 11/19, 1989

**The turkey pardon started as a way to dodge a question.**

George H. W. Bush gave the first official pardon on November 17, 1989. Reagan had used the word 2 years earlier, about a turkey called Charlie, while being asked whether he would pardon 2 men over Iran-Contra.

*The tradition started as a way of changing the subject.*

Sound: 1980s light orchestral, warm strings

- White House records of the 1989 presidential turkey presentation

## 20. Fri 11/20, 1620s

**They never called themselves Pilgrims.**

They called themselves Saints. Other colonists called them Old Comers, or the Forefathers. The capital P name caught on after Daniel Webster said Pilgrim Fathers at the 1820 bicentennial, about 200 years after the landing.

*The name arrived 200 years late, in a speech, from a man who never met them.*

Sound: one plucked string instrument, sparse

- William Bradford's Of Plymouth Plantation; the popularisation of Pilgrims in 19th century oratory

---

## 21. Sat 11/21, pre-1500s

**Corn, beans and squash were planted as 1 system.**

Corn gives the beans something to climb. The beans host rhizobia on their roots, which pull nitrogen out of the air into the soil, and the vines hold the corn steady in wind. The squash leaves shade the ground, keep the water in and crowd the weeds out. 3 crops, 1 mound, almost no tending.

*3 crops doing one another's maintenance.*

Sound: warm modern instrumental, open and slow

- documented Haudenosaunee and wider Indigenous companion planting; nitrogen fixation in legumes

## 22. Sun 11/22, no date

**Leaves do not fall. They get pushed.**

The tree builds the breaking point in spring, while it is still growing: an abscission layer at the base of every leaf stalk. In autumn the auxin stops flowing, cells in that layer elongate, and the weak walled cells above them snap. The leaf is released on a schedule set months earlier.

*The tree decided in April how this week would end.*

Sound: warm modern instrumental, open and slow

- plant physiology on the abscission layer, auxin withdrawal and cell elongation

## 23. Mon 11/23, 1663 to now

**The language spoken at that harvest had no speakers left. It has them again.**

Nobody is recorded speaking Wôpanâak after about 1833. In 1993 Jessie Little Doe Baird began rebuilding it from what had been written down: the 1663 Eliot Bible, the first complete Bible printed in this hemisphere, and deeds and wills written by speakers themselves. It is being taught in Wampanoag households now.

*The people at that harvest are not a historical detail. They are a nation, and they got their language back out of a book.*

Sound: 1 plucked string, sparse. No recording of the language exists from 1663, which is the fact

- Wôpanâak Language Reclamation Project; the 1663 Eliot Bible, the first complete Bible printed in this hemisphere; Baird MacArthur Fellowship 2010

## 24. Tue 11/24, 2005

**Gratitude journaling works better once a week than 3 times a week.**

In a 2005 study, people who wrote down what they were grateful for once a week measurably improved. The group doing it 3 times a week got no benefit, and some came out slightly worse. Doing it more often turned noticing into a chore.

*This one fails by being overdone, not by being skipped.*

Sound: warm modern instrumental, open and slow

- Lyubomirsky, Sheldon and Schkade 2005, once a week against 3 times a week. Effect sizes in this literature are modest and not every measure moves, so the caption claims the frequency finding and nothing more

## 25. Wed 11/25, 1863

**The most quoted Thanksgiving document was not written by the man who signed it.**

Lincoln set the last Thursday in November on October 3, 1863, months after Gettysburg. He did not write it. The proclamation is in Secretary of State William Seward's handwriting, which Lincoln's own secretary John Nicolay confirmed in a letter in 1864.

*The words everybody quotes at this table were written by the man nobody quotes.*

Sound: sparse piano, 1860s parlour

- Lincoln's proclamation of October 3, 1863

## 26. Thu 11/26, 1621

**No gifts. No costumes. Just the table.**

Every other holiday on the calendar wants you to buy something or wear something. This 1 asks you to turn up and eat with people.

*That is the whole assignment.*

Sound: warm modern instrumental, open and slow

- none. This is a closing thought and not a factual claim
